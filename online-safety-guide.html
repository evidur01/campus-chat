Online Safety Guide for Anonymous Chats

Practical ways to protect your identity, recognize manipulation and respond to uncomfortable conversations online.

Before you start a conversation

Anonymous conversations can be enjoyable because there is less pressure to introduce yourself in a formal way. But anonymity also means a stranger's stated age, school, name, or intentions may not be accurate. Treat a new chat as a first meeting with someone you have not verified, even when the conversation feels friendly.

Start with low-stakes subjects such as music, books, study habits or hobbies. You can participate without disclosing your full name, birthday, student number, phone number or exact location. A nickname is enough for many conversations.

Keep identifying details out of the chat

Small facts can identify someone when combined. A school, course, year level, class schedule and favorite study spot might make it possible to find a person offline. Think about the combination of details you have shared rather than evaluating each detail on its own.

Avoid sharing passwords, one-time passwords, payment account details, identification documents, private photos and screenshots of messages containing other people's information. If a stranger asks to move to another app, remember that your new profile might reveal your name, connections or location.

Recognize pressure and scams

A common warning sign is urgency: someone insists you send money immediately, prove your identity, share a verification code or follow an unfamiliar link. Other warning signs include offers that sound too good to be true and attempts to make you feel guilty for setting boundaries.

Do not open suspicious downloads, send money to a stranger, or use your accounts to receive and forward someone else's payments. You do not owe an explanation for ending a chat that feels unsafe. A respectful conversation allows you to say no.

What to do when a conversation goes wrong

On Campus Konek, use Skip to leave the conversation and Report to submit a concern. If you report behavior, describe what happened factually. Do not assume a report will receive an immediate response; avoid continuing the exchange while waiting.

If you are threatened, extorted, or concerned about an immediate danger, prioritize your safety and contact an appropriate local authority or emergency service. Preserve relevant evidence only if doing so is safe and lawful. You can contact the Campus Konek operator at support@campuskonek.com for website-related safety concerns.

Make your own safety routine

Before you chat, decide what you are willing to share and what will make you leave. During a conversation, pause before opening links or sending photos. After a conversation, review what you revealed and remove personal details from other public profiles if needed.

Campus Konek is intended for adults aged 18 or older. An age confirmation checkbox is not identity verification, so users should not rely on it as proof of another visitor's age or identity.
