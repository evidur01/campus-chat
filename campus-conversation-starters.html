Conversation Starters for College Students

Thoughtful conversation prompts for meeting new people online without revealing sensitive personal information.

Start easy and allow room to answer

A good opening question should be easy to answer without revealing private information. Try asking what song someone has on repeat, what skill they wish they could learn, or which fictional world they would visit for a day. Questions like these create opportunities for follow-up without asking someone to identify themselves.

If the other person responds briefly, avoid treating it as a challenge. You can offer your own answer first and let the conversation develop naturally. For example: 'I have been learning to cook simple meals. Is there a dish you want to master?'

Five campus-friendly topics

Study routines: 'Are you more productive early in the morning or late at night?' Hobbies: 'What do you do when you have a free afternoon?' Music: 'What is a song you never get tired of?' Food: 'What is your go-to budget meal?' Everyday wins: 'What small thing made your week better?'

These questions work whether the other person attends the same school, another college or is simply interested in campus life. Avoid asking for exact class times or a person's usual route home.

Go beyond yes-or-no questions

Questions beginning with 'what', 'how' or 'why' often lead to richer conversations than questions that can be answered with one word. After someone shares a hobby, ask what they enjoy about it or how they got interested. Listen to the answer instead of immediately changing the topic.

You can also share harmless preferences: favorite fictional characters, comfort food, or a small creative project. Try to balance curiosity with reciprocity: answer some of your own questions, but never pressure someone to share more than they want.

When the conversation slows down

Silence is normal. Rather than sending many messages in a row, try a new light topic: 'Would you rather learn an instrument or a new language?' If the other person seems uninterested or uncomfortable, respect that and consider ending the conversation.

The goal is not to impress a stranger at all costs. The most useful conversation skill is noticing whether both people want to continue.

Respect boundaries

Campus Konek connects strangers anonymously. Do not ask for passwords, identifying documents, financial details, private photos or live locations. Never assume someone's nickname or school is verified. If a person says they are uncomfortable, change the topic or end the conversation.

If a chat turns into harassment or coercion, use the Skip and Report options. You can reach the website's support contact at support@campuskonek.com for additional site-related concerns.
