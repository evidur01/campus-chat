Digital Privacy Checklist for Students

A practical checklist for limiting accidental oversharing in online student communities.

Understand your digital footprint

Digital privacy is not just about hiding a password. Online profiles, posted photos, public comments and screenshots can reveal patterns about where you study, who you spend time with and where you go regularly. On anonymous chat sites, a few casual facts can sometimes connect an alias to a real identity.

The easiest place to start is reviewing what strangers can see about you without signing in. Search for your public usernames, look at old biographies and check whether photos reveal addresses, school IDs or location details.

Review social-account settings

Check whether your social profiles disclose your birthday, phone number, friends list or public posts. Limit who can message you and who can find your account by contact details where your chosen platform provides those controls.

If you use the same nickname on many services, people may connect those accounts. A distinct alias for casual chats can make unwanted cross-platform searches harder. It cannot guarantee anonymity, but it reduces one simple way to identify you.

Think before sharing a screenshot

Screenshots may contain notifications, usernames, timestamps and private messages that were not intended to be shared. Crop or redact sensitive details before posting, and ask permission before reposting someone else's conversation.

A photo can also reveal more than its main subject: a visible ID card, a building sign or a document on a desk may disclose personal details. When in doubt, leave the photo out.

Protect accounts and devices

Use a unique password for important accounts and turn on multi-factor authentication where available. Do not share one-time codes, even when someone claims to be support staff. Keep your browser and device software updated and review connected apps you no longer use.

Avoid opening suspicious files or links from strangers. If you think an account has been compromised, change its password using the official app or website and review active sessions.

Privacy in anonymous chat

Campus Konek allows an optional nickname and school. Neither is required for a meaningful conversation. Think carefully before revealing exact academic schedules, home addresses or contact details. The platform's age confirmation is not identity verification.

Messages and submissions may remain in service databases after a conversation ends; do not treat anonymous chat as automatically disappearing or end-to-end encrypted. For data questions or requests, email support@campuskonek.com. Consult the Privacy Policy for current information.
